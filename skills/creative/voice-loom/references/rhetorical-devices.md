# Rhetorical Device Library

Voice Loom's Shakespeare layer draws from classical rhetoric. Twelve devices the skill can suggest per statement. Not every statement needs a device. Suggest one only when the statement's structure creates a natural opening. A forced device is worse than no device.

---

## 1. Antithesis

**Definition:** Contrast two ideas in parallel grammatical structure.

**Effect:** Sharpens a comparison by setting opposites against each other.

**Example:**
- Statement: "The tools are fast but careless."
- Antithesis: "Fast at typing, slow at thinking."

**When to use:** When a statement contains a natural opposition — speed vs quality, power vs control, confidence vs humility.

**AI version (weak):** "While the tools are fast, they sometimes make mistakes."
**Human version (strong):** "Fast hands. No brain."

---

## 2. Anaphora

**Definition:** Repeat the same word or phrase at the start of successive clauses.

**Effect:** Builds momentum and emotional intensity through repetition.

**Example:**
- Statement: "Every team hits the same wall with AI adoption."
- Anaphora: "Every team. Every sprint. Same wall."

**When to use:** When you want to hammer a point, build toward a climax, or create rhythm through repetition.

**AI version (weak):** "Many teams encounter similar challenges when adopting AI tools."
**Human version (strong):** "Same hype. Same pilot. Same graveyard."

---

## 3. Chiasmus

**Definition:** Reverse the word order in the second of two parallel phrases (AB to BA).

**Effect:** Makes a line stick in memory through symmetrical reversal.

**Example:**
- Statement: "AI can help you write, but writing helps you think."
- Chiasmus: "AI writes fast. Thinking writes deep."

**When to use:** When a statement has two concepts that can be reversed to reveal a deeper truth.

**AI version (weak):** "Writing and thinking have a reciprocal relationship."
**Human version (strong):** "Write to think. Not think to write."

---

## 4. Litotes

**Definition:** Affirm something by negating its opposite (understatement).

**Effect:** Understated confidence. The point lands harder for being undersold.

**Example:**
- Statement: "The results were surprisingly good."
- Litotes: "The results were not bad."

**When to use:** When confidence is high but you want to avoid bragging. Also for dry humor.

**AI version (weak):** "The outcome exceeded expectations."
**Human version (strong):** "We did not break anything. I am calling that a win."

---

## 5. Zeugma

**Definition:** One verb governs two or more objects in different senses.

**Effect:** Surprise and wit from a single verb pulling double duty.

**Example:**
- Statement: "She lost her keys and her patience."
- Zeugma: "She left the meeting with a decision and a headache."

**When to use:** When a statement can pivot from literal to figurative with a single verb. Use sparingly — one per piece maximum.

**AI version (weak):** "She became both frustrated and unable to find her keys."
**Human version (strong):** "She left the meeting with a promotion and a migraine."

---

## 6. Assonance

**Definition:** Repeat vowel sounds within words in close proximity.

**Effect:** Subtle musicality that makes prose feel crafted without calling attention to itself.

**Example:**
- Statement: "The slow code review process drains momentum."
- Assonance: "The slow code flow drains the soul."

**When to use:** When a statement feels flat and needs sonic texture. Assonance should be felt, not noticed.

**AI version (weak):** "The code review process reduces momentum over time."
**Human version (strong):** "Slow code rots more than syntax. It rots morale."

---

## 7. Polysyndeton

**Definition:** Deliberately use extra conjunctions (and, or, but) where grammar would normally omit them.

**Effect:** Breathless accumulation. Feels like the thought is unfolding in real time.

**Example:**
- Statement: "The pipeline handles ingestion, transformation, validation, delivery."
- Polysyndeton: "Ingest and transform and validate and ship and pray and repeat."

**When to use:** When you want the reader to feel the weight or volume of something.

**AI version (weak):** "The pipeline manages multiple stages from ingestion through delivery."
**Human version (strong):** "Ingest and transform and validate and ship and pray and repeat."

---

## 8. Asyndeton

**Definition:** Deliberately omit conjunctions between items in a series.

**Effect:** Stark, fast, emphatic. Each item lands like a punch.

**Example:**
- Statement: "We tested, iterated, shipped, and repeated."
- Asyndeton: "Test. Ship. Repeat. No meetings in between."

**When to use:** When you want urgency or finality. The opposite of polysyndeton. Choose one per paragraph, never both.

**AI version (weak):** "The team went through multiple cycles of testing, iteration, and shipping."
**Human version (strong):** "Test. Ship. Repeat. No meetings."

---

## 9. Epistrophe

**Definition:** Repeat the same word or phrase at the end of successive clauses.

**Effect:** Hammers a point by ending every clause on the same note.

**Example:**
- Statement: "Built by engineers, tested by engineers, broken by engineers."
- Epistrophe: "Code written by humans. Reviewed by humans. Deployed by humans. Broken by humans."

**When to use:** When a single word or phrase is the central theme and you want it to land repeatedly.

**AI version (weak):** "Engineers are responsible for building, testing, and debugging the system."
**Human version (strong):** "Written by humans. Reviewed by humans. Broken by humans."

---

## 10. Enallage

**Definition:** Intentional grammatical shift — wrong tense, wrong number, wrong person — for effect.

**Effect:** Creates voice and personality by breaking grammar deliberately. Feels conversational.

**Example:**
- Statement: "We do not have that feature yet."
- Enallage: "We ain't got that feature yet. But we will."

**When to use:** When the medium allows personality. Never in formal documentation. One per piece maximum.

**AI version (weak):** "That feature is not currently available."
**Human version (strong):** "That feature? Not yet. We are working on it."

---

## 11. Auxesis

**Definition:** Arrange words or ideas in ascending order of importance or magnitude.

**Effect:** Builds toward a climax. The reader feels the escalation.

**Example:**
- Statement: "First it was a side project, then a team, then a product."
- Auxesis: "A script. A repo. A team. A product. A company."

**When to use:** When telling a progression story — growth, escalation, accumulation.

**AI version (weak):** "The project grew from a small experiment into a full product."
**Human version (strong):** "One notebook. Three engineers. Twelve thousand users. No going back."

---

## 12. Metanoia

**Definition:** Correct or qualify a statement mid-sentence as if thinking in real time.

**Effect:** Authentic thinking on the page. The reader sees you change your mind.

**Example:**
- Statement: "The feature works. Actually, it works most of the time."
- Metanoia: "We shipped it. It works. Well. It works well enough that nobody has complained. Yet."

**When to use:** When vulnerability or intellectual honesty matters. Builds trust by showing the thought process, not just the conclusion.

**AI version (weak):** "The feature has some limitations in edge cases."
**Human version (strong):** "We shipped it. It works. Well — it works for the happy path. The edge cases are another story."

---

## Usage Rules

1. **One device per statement maximum.** Never layer multiple devices on one statement.
2. **Suggest, do not force.** If a device does not fit naturally, leave the row bare. Better bare than tortured.
3. **Calibrate to the medium.** More devices for LinkedIn and pitch decks. None for API docs.
4. **The device serves meaning, not the other way.** If applying a device changes the meaning, skip it.
5. **Vary devices across rows.** Do not use antithesis three rows in a row.
